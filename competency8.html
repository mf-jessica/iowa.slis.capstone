Social Justice

My preliminary Grant Proposal for an imagined digital community archive project for SLIS 5400: Community Archives aligns with the ALA Core Competence on Social Justice. My imagined archive, CACAO (Capoeira America Community Archive Online), serves as a centralized hub for preserving the intangible and ephemeral traditions of US capoeiristas. This project demonstrates how to transition a conceptual blueprint into an operational archival project that serves an underdocumented community.

Through this assignment, I recognized and intentionally challenged dominant archival systems that often marginalize decentralized Afro-Brazilian cultural expressions (8B). I also designed a social justice framework that relies on outreach and partnerships, using a Community Advisory Board to ensure the community retains participation in and control over its narrative (8D). I learned that addressing archival silences requires moving beyond institutional ownership models toward stewardship, in which the archive serves as a digital steward rather than a proprietor.

This project has influenced my professional growth by teaching me to balance theoretical activism with the realistic constraints of labor and infrastructure. To improve my future practice, I will consider how to lower barriers for marginalized groups to document their own experiences in real time, such as through mobile digitization kits. I will prioritize building inclusive collections that recognize practitioners of a tradition as the rightful authorities over their own historical records.
