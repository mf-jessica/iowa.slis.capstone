2. Information Resources

My Selection and Deselection Manifesto for SLIS 6330: Collection Development demonstrates competency in ALA's Core Competence for Information Resources. This assignment shows my understanding of a variety of information resources, practices, and frameworks in a public library context, gained through coursework and my work experience as a Teen Reference Librarian.

Each tenet of the manifesto encompasses some aspect of the collection management lifecycle, from the philosophy and policy behind acquisition and selection decisions to the frameworks guiding de-selection and weeding of materials (2B). The document also considers materials in different formats and genres, such as print collections and digital subscriptions (2A), and how this affects patrons' access to materials and their ability to exercise their right to intellectual freedom. Another consideration is ensuring robust, culturally responsive circulation data so the library's collection reflects our communities' needs while continuing to acquire new materials that reflect diverse voices and experiences (2C).

I drafted my manifesto from concepts and frameworks in foundational texts about weeding, collection development and management, and my library district's Collection Development Policy. Because of how our departments are structured, I have little say in these processes at my library. Weeding lists are automated and sent to us via an "intelligent material management system." Moving forward, I want more opportunities to get involved in collection management, learn how our Collection Librarians make decisions, and find out whether any internal, unwritten selection and deselection manifestos exist on their team.

Professional Development

For this competency area, I identified two professional development opportunities that would strengthen my understanding of how libraries expand access to information resources beyond their own collections. A course like Library Juice Academy's Introduction to Interlibrary Loan will help me understand resource-sharing processes and how libraries locate and provide materials they do not own. ALA's on-demand webinar, Partnering with the National Library Service to Provide Materials and Services to People with Print Disabilities, will teach me more about accessible reading materials and services for people with print disabilities and how to connect patrons with those services. Together, these trainings will help me better understand how libraries can meet diverse patron needs through resource sharing, accessible formats, and partnerships.
