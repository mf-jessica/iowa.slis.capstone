Organization of Recorded Knowledge and Information

My Digital Collection Project for SLIS 5030: Information Organization, the Lela Florence Steele Archives, features digitized correspondence and newspaper clippings from 1973-1974 and aligns with the ALA Core Competence on Organization of Recorded Knowledge and Information. This competence highlights principles and tools for organizing information, which I applied to create an accessible, discoverable online collection using metadata (5A).

If the PDF Viewer doesn't render for you, view my metadata spreadsheet here.

A crucial part of the organization process involved creating metadata for each item. I analyzed the content of documents to extract key descriptive elements, such as dates, subjects (e.g., "Families--United States--Correspondence," "Watergate Affair, 1972-1974--Public opinion"), people mentioned, and locations, and administrative elements like object ID, format, and file name. This required careful reading and critical thinking to accurately represent the documents' content with consistent terminology (5B). The result is a structured dataset that lets users browse and search the collection effectively, demonstrating the practical application of organizational principles and descriptive skills.

Through this project, I recognized the critical importance of robust metadata for information discovery. Analyzing the letters and clippings provided hands-on experience with the challenges of describing a collection of resources. I learned that effective organization requires both technical skills and strong analytical abilities. I plan to connect with collection librarians and copy catalogers in my district to pursue a shadowing opportunity that will deepen my understanding of how their work influences material access in our branches and service areas. In the future, I will continue to explore cataloging and metadata standards (5C, 5D) to improve information access for users.

Professional Development

Completing a course like the Society of American Archivists' Introduction to Processing Digital Records and Manuscripts will help me develop my skills in this competency area. I'm particularly interested in expanding the Lela Florence Steele Archives I created for my Information Organization course. My mother has dozens more letters from family members dating back to the US Civil War. I hope to build a more comprehensive digital collection that preserves these historic materials and makes them accessible and discoverable. This training will strengthen my understanding of how to process, organize, describe, and preserve archival materials for long-term access. Beyond applying these skills to my personal project, I will also be better equipped to assist patrons with digitization and preservation projects at our library's Digital Media Stations.
