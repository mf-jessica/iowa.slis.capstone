Reference and User Services

My assignment to Compare Two LIS Databases for SLIS 6115: Information and Inquiry aligns closely with ALA's Core Competence on Reference and User Services, which highlights essential knowledge, skills, and attitudes needed to provide effective reference assistance. This competency encompasses abilities such as discovering and retrieving information (6A), conducting consultation and instruction (6B), and practicing cultural humility and equity (6F), all of which I have practiced and reflected upon.

This assignment helped me hone my skills in information discovery and retrieval (6A) as I analyzed different information tools and matched them to user needs. By comparing LISTA and LISA, I learned to discern which database is better suited for theoretical versus applied research questions, an insight that improves my ability to consult effectively and guide users to the most useful resources (6B). This competency in user-centered guidance is central to the human element of reference services, where understanding the context and purpose behind an inquiry informs how I recommend resources.

Lifelong learning is essential in Reference and User Services, and I have mapped out several concrete steps toward ongoing professional development. I joined the Reference and User Services Association (RUSA) to engage with peers, stay current with best practices, and deepen my expertise through networking and continuing education. I will seek out training, webinars, and workshops to familiarize myself with databases and other resources, particularly in specialized subject areas like legal or medical reference, where I have knowledge gaps.

Professional Development

Joining a committee within the Reference Services Section of RUSA would enable me to collaborate with colleagues across library systems, learn about emerging practices in reference and user services, and help develop resources for library professionals. Volunteer applications typically open in the spring, so I have time to consider which committee. I'm leaning toward the Reference Services in Public Libraries committee, since providing reference services in a public library is a big part of my job, or the Library Service to an Aging Population committee because older adults are a population whose needs are often not prioritized at my branch, especially older adults with disabilities like low vision. I would use this opportunity to strengthen my approach to community information needs, evaluate reference services, and provide responsive, inclusive assistance to patrons.
