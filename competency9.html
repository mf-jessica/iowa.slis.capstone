Technological Knowledge and Skills

The AI Policy group assignment for SLIS 6335: Metadata Theories and Application focused on developing a practical and forward-thinking approach to generative AI in metadata creation, aligning with the ALA Core Competence on Technological Knowledge and Skills. Our goal was to create a policy that balanced the benefits of emerging technologies with ethical considerations and organizational sustainability. By outlining appropriate uses and verification workflows, we addressed how technology can enhance library services while maintaining accuracy and integrity.

This project reinforced the importance of navigating the ethical and cultural impacts (9B) of implementing AI tools. While generative AI improves efficiency in metadata creation, we recognized its limitations, such as hallucinations and bias, particularly when generating descriptive metadata. To mitigate this, we required oversight by trained librarians to ensure accuracy and protect the library's reputation. Our policy also prohibited inputting patron information into AI systems to safeguard user trust. Our work emphasized the need to evaluate emerging technologies continuously (9C). Discussions around AI's alignment with library goals highlighted that new tools must support accessibility. A key challenge arose when a group member raised late-stage concerns about our policy direction, underscoring the importance of creating space for nuanced conversations early in the decision-making process.

This experience reinforced the need to balance innovation with thoughtful evaluation and collaboration. In future professional practice, I will prioritize proactive dialogue with stakeholders, establish clear project parameters early, and continually assess the impacts of emerging technologies to support responsible adoption that effectively serves libraries and communities.

Professional Development

On November 11, 2025, I attended a live online session of the SLIS Professional Development Series, Professional Growth Through Personal Engagement, featuring Dr. Casey Fiesler on "The Librarian's Dividend: Pathways Towards Information Ethics and Literacy in the Age of Generative AI." I saw Dr. Fiesler speak at my library district's Staff Day event several weeks prior, so I was excited to attend another of her talks. In her SLIS presentation, Dr. Fiesler defined malinformation and information disorder and discussed when to use LLMs versus a search engine, as well as the bias and ethical quandaries inherent in generative AI technologies.

3 Takeaways

  1. "The liar's dividend" is the benefit that liars receive from existing in a world in which it is unclear what is true and what is false (and therefore one can claim anything is false). The challenge is that this problem is so difficult to address. When you can't believe what you see, your only option is to take the time to track down the original source, which takes work, skills, time, and persistence. Source credibility and awareness of potential fakery are now the most essential considerations.
  2. "The librarian's dividend" is the social and ethical benefit that comes from access to people and institutions who can help us (and teach us how to) evaluate information. Librarians and teachers are well positioned to talk with people about the limitations, risks, and ethical concerns of generative AI.
  3. Often when people say "AI literacy," they mean prompt engineering or how to use generative AI. What Dr. Fiesler means by AI literacy is to understand how it works. You have to know why the information you get out of it might be incorrect.

2 Questions

  1. Dr. Fiesler said something like, "You must be able to determine the appropriate risk tolerance for any use of AI." How are libraries determining the appropriate risk tolerance for the ways their staff is using AI at work? How are librarians teaching AI risk tolerance to patrons?
  2. Can library staff ethically refuse to learn about or engage with generative AI and still be able to teach patrons about it? How can we provide equitable access to information about a topic or resource without understanding it ourselves?

1 Thing

One thing I discovered thanks to this session is Dr. Fiesler's AI Ethics Syllabus. It's almost an annotated bibliography in which she links to videos and articles organized into thematic sections. After this session, I have continued to seek out learning opportunities about generative AI in general and AI in librarianship. I've attended more webinars on the topic, and I'm currently reading Mastering AI: A Survival Guide to Our Superpowered Future by Jeremy Kahn (2024).
