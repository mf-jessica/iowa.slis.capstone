4. Management and Administration

The Evidence-Based Practice Skill Builder from SLIS 6170: Management, Teams, and Leadership aligns with the ALA Core Competence on Management and Administration, which calls on library professionals to work with managers "to ensure that the information setting meets the needs of the community," through a tween/teen services lens. This assignment guided my group through the process of formulating a clear question using the SPICE framework, conducting an organizational scan via SOAR analysis, evaluating evidence using the CRAAP Test, and synthesizing that evidence to identify needs, specifically creating a tween space and programs.

If the PDF Viewer doesn't render for you, view my Evidence-Based Practice Skill Builder here.

This foundational work directly contributed to understanding how to assess needs and evaluate potential outcomes (4C). The assignment required setting measurable goals and objectives (SMART objectives) and outlining data collection methods for measurement. This structured approach to defining project goals and planning for evaluation directly addresses the planning aspect of project management (4F). Working through these frameworks provided a practical application of theoretical concepts and simulated a real-world planning scenario.

To continue developing in these areas, I plan to apply these skills in my current Teen Reference Librarian role by incorporating measurable objectives into future programming plans and using data (like attendance or feedback) to assess success (4C). I will use project management frameworks, such as scoping and defining deliverables, to support clearer planning for initiatives I lead (4F). I am interested in exploring training in specific assessment tools like Project Outcome and project management software like Monday.com to enhance my technical skills in these areas.

Professional Development

On Thursday, April 2, 2026, I attended a session at the Public Library Association's national conference in Minneapolis, Minnesota, titled From Rules of Conduct to Community Use Agreements: A Case Study in Re-Designing Rules to Reflect Library Values, presented by Valerie Wonder, Micah Scheff, Richard Counsil, and McCall Olsen from Seattle Public Library. I chose this session because staff at my library branch, along with most of the district's teen services librarians, have been advocating for years for changes to our Patron Code of Conduct and enforcement processes. The Seattle team shared valuable insights from their experience, focusing on intentionality, rebuilding trust with adversely impacted communities, and shifting to a system that empowers and trusts branch staff.

PLA 2026!

3 Takeaways

  1. Rigid, consistent enforcement of rules of conduct regardless of context or intent perpetuates a White Supremacy kind of harm on people, and unintentionally excludes specific groups of people, especially those who are already vulnerable. There's almost always an unmet need behind a behavior.
  2. Public libraries are contested spaces that require collective regulation. The Community Use Agreement has three categories: Be Safe, Be Respectful, Be Aware. The desired outcomes are to build relationships, present options and boundaries first, and reduce the likelihood, duration, and frequency of suspensions.
  3. The values of "caring, curiosity, and belonging" ask staff to exercise judgment, stay curious, build rapport, and have developmentally appropriate conversations from a place of humility.

2 Questions

  1. How does such a large library district give all branch staff the authority to suspend patrons for up to two weeks without administrative approval? What do the logistics of that look like? How are those suspensions documented and communicated district-wide? How is the patron informed--the same or different from longer suspensions?
  2. Seattle's Community Use Agreement uses simple, plain language so all patrons can understand it. Why is there so much resistance to adopting plain language in our Patron Code of Conduct? Why such devotion to hundreds of words of legalese?

1 Thing

One thing I did after returning to work from PLA 2026 was loudly and animatedly talk about this session with my manager and every Safety Specialist who came through my branch. I shared the slides and photos of my handwritten notes with my manager and drew parallels between what Seattle Public Library did and what our branch staff tried to do by drafting "Youth Friendly Library Rules" (which we were then forbidden to use). I asked if we could stop using the word "banned" and change it to "suspended," as Seattle changed "exclusion" to "suspension," because language matters.

Incidentally, a few weeks later, the Seattle Public Library team did an encore of this presentation as a webinar. My manager and the Safety manager attended. Suddenly, a Decision Announcement came out that we would no longer be "banning" patrons, we would be "suspending" them. A project group was formed, including my manager, the Safety manager, and others to examine our Patron Code of Conduct and revise it to be less rigid and more equitable. That work is ongoing.
