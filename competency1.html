1. Gateway Knowledge

My "casting off essay" for SLIS 5010: Libraries, Culture, and Society exemplifies my engagement with the ALA Gateway Knowledge Competence. This paper explores the intersection of library policies, restorative justice practices, and how public libraries treat youth patrons, particularly teens of color. It embodies key competencies, including the ethics and values of librarianship (1A), democratic principles and intellectual freedom (1B), and the historical and social contexts of libraries (1C, 1F). This assignment allowed me to develop and demonstrate my ability to analyze complex problems and propose collaborative solutions (1I) while engaging in effective written communication (1J).

For this paper, I examined how teens, particularly those from marginalized communities, often face over-policing and exclusion in library spaces. This process required deep engagement with foundational principles of the library profession, such as equity of access and intellectual freedom (1A, 1B). I traced libraries’ historical role as both sites of inclusion and exclusion (1C). I analyzed current trends, such as the rise of restorative justice practices, to frame my arguments within a broader social and cultural context (1F). By critically evaluating library policies and case studies, I identified barriers that hinder equitable access for teens and proposed practical strategies to create more inclusive environments (1I).

This research shaped my professional perspective and future practice, reinforcing that libraries are dynamic institutions that must continually adapt to meet the needs of diverse patron populations. As a result, I have developed a more nuanced understanding of how libraries can serve as catalysts for social change.
